Sorrel vs Speechify

A $9.99 one-time Mac utility next to a recurring cross-device reading platform — so you can tell which one you actually need.

The short version

They're not really the same kind of product. Speechify is a full reading platform for books, PDFs, AI voices, and multiple devices, sold on recurring plans. Sorrel is a small, private Mac utility: paste copied text into Sorrel and your Mac reads it aloud with macOS system voices, for $9.99 once.

If you want a broad reading platform across all your devices, Speechify may be the better fit. If you mostly want to listen to AI answers, articles, and messy text on your Mac — without a subscription and, with your Mac's system voices, with reading text processed locally — that's exactly what Sorrel is built for.

Which should you pick?

An honest recommendation

Pick Sorrel if…

  • You mostly read on a Mac
  • You want to listen to Claude / ChatGPT answers, docs and articles
  • You'd rather pay once than subscribe
  • You care about privacy — with macOS system voices, reading text stays on your Mac
  • Your Mac's built-in voices are good enough for you

Pick Speechify if…

  • You want a large premium AI voice library
  • You read whole books, PDFs and Kindle in one library
  • You need it on iPhone, Windows and the web too
  • You're fine with a yearly subscription
  • You're comfortable with provider-backed cloud processing
Sorrel's home turf

Made for listening to AI answers

Sorrel shines in a place selection-based tools can struggle: copied AI replies.

⌥

Works where selection is awkward

Most readers start with selected text. In the Claude and ChatGPT apps, where selection can be awkward, Sorrel reads your clipboard — paste into the app, or use the optional hotkey.

✦

Hears the answer, not the symbols

It strips markdown, code blocks, links and footnotes before speaking, so a long AI reply sounds like a person talking — not a wall of formatting.

🔒

Private by default

With system voices, playback is generated on your Mac. No Sorrel account is required for reading, and reading text is not uploaded to Sorrel.

When Speechify is the better choice: if you want to listen to entire books and long PDFs in premium AI voices across your phone, Windows PC and the web, Speechify does that and Sorrel doesn't. Sorrel is deliberately small — a fast, private way to listen on a Mac, not a cross-device library.
FAQ

Common questions

Is Sorrel a good Speechify alternative?

It depends what you need. If you wanted Speechify for a private, pay-once way to listen to AI answers, articles and messy text on your Mac, Sorrel is a focused, cheaper fit. If you wanted a large AI voice library and whole-book reading across devices, Sorrel isn't trying to replace that.

Does Sorrel have AI voices like Speechify?

No. Sorrel uses macOS system voices, including voices macOS exposes to third-party apps. That's the trade for staying private and subscription-free on the system-voice path. If you opt into a cloud voice with your own key, filtered text goes to that provider.

Is Sorrel really one-time, with no subscription?

Yes. It's free for 14 days, then $9.99 once. No subscription and no recurring charge — use it on the Macs you own.

Can Sorrel read ChatGPT and Claude answers aloud?

Yes. Because it reads your clipboard, it works anywhere you can copy text, including the Claude and ChatGPT desktop apps, and it strips the markdown and code first.

Is my text private with Sorrel?

With system-voice playback, audio is generated on your Mac and reading text is not uploaded to Sorrel. Optional cloud voices send filtered text to the provider you choose, and purchase or license activation can contact Sorrel without sending reading text.
